What We Build

What we build for dealerships

Every engagement starts with how your store actually runs — your accounts, your pay plans, your DMS. We design and build systems that answer the questions your current reporting cannot.

Dealership Dashboard Platform

A role-gated web dashboard covering the whole store or group: consolidated P&L, variable and fixed operations, scenario modeling, and generated reporting. Owners see everything; lenders and capital partners see a curated subset. Learn more →

F&I & Fixed Operations Analytics

Product penetration, per-copy gross, lender spread and reinsurance on the variable side. Absorption, effective labor rate, technician and advisor productivity, parts and warranty on the fixed side — per store, per person. Learn more →

DMS & CRM Integration

Extracts from CDK, Reynolds, Dealertrack, Tekion, VinSolutions and the rest; account structures normalized across rooftops; everything reconciled to the financial statement in a warehouse you own. Learn more →

Used Vehicle & Recon Analytics

True days in inventory, landed cost with reconditioning charged back to the buyer who sourced the unit, staged recon cycle time, merchandising readiness, and a wholesale post-mortem that informs buying. Learn more →

Workflow & Document Automation

Documents rendered from live data, ESIGN-compliant signature and countersignature, PDF form fill, approval routing, deal jacket completeness, and month-end packages generated on a schedule. Learn more →

Acquisition & Diligence Systems

The Platform

Built for how a dealership
actually operates

Dealerships run on a stack that was never designed to talk to itself: a DMS, a CRM, a desking tool, an inventory feed, a reinsurance statement, and a folder of month-end spreadsheets. The numbers exist. They arrive late, they disagree across rooftops, and someone rebuilds them by hand every period.

We build the layer that closes that gap — and we build it around your operation rather than configuring you into a product. The modules below are what we have built and what we build from. Which of them you get, and how they are wired to your data, is settled during assessment.

How We Work

A clear path from the statement
to a system people use

01

Assess

We sit with the statement, the DMS, and the people who produce today's reports. Which numbers are trusted, which are rebuilt by hand, and which nobody can reproduce.

02

Define

Every metric gets one written definition and one benchmark. This is the step most projects skip, and it is why most dealer dashboards end up disputed rather than used.

03

Build

Extracts, reconciliation, then dashboards — shipped in iterative releases. The first release has one job: match the numbers you already trust.

04

Improve

Nightly loads, monitoring, and the review cadence the reporting feeds. A scorecard with no meeting attached stops being read within a month.

Outcomes

Reporting that changes
what happens on Monday

A dashboard is worth what its worst number is worth. These are the outcomes we design for, and the limits we state plainly.

Time

End the month-end assembly

The reporting package, the DOC report, and the recurring lender and OEM submissions generated from reconciled data instead of rebuilt by hand.

Cadence

See the store daily, not monthly

Gross, inventory age, and absorption available the morning after, so a bad week is visible in the week it happens rather than three weeks later.

Consistency

Compare rooftops honestly

One chart of accounts and one set of definitions across every store, so an outlier is a question about the store rather than about the mapping.

Diagnosis

Name the constraint

Break an average down far enough that it points at something a manager can change. The expensive answer is frequently the wrong one.

Speed

Answer partners in the meeting

A what-if is a lever move and a model re-run rather than two days of spreadsheet work after the fact.

Honesty

Say what cannot be computed

Where a figure cannot be produced reliably, the system reports the limit instead of estimating. One quiet guess costs more credibility than a dozen honest blanks.
