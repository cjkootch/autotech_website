Dealership Workflow & Document Automation

Every store has a set of tasks that exist only because a system does not talk to another system: the retyped form, the rebuilt spreadsheet, the deal jacket chased across three desks, the report assembled by hand every month. We remove them one at a time, starting with the ones that cost the most hours.

The work nobody has a line item for

Ask a dealership controller how many hours a month go into assembling the reporting package, and the answer is usually a shrug and a number that turns out to be low. Ask a title clerk how many forms carry data that already exists somewhere in the DMS, and the answer is most of them.

None of this shows up as a problem, because nobody is failing at it. It gets done. It just gets done by people, repeatedly, at a cost the store has stopped noticing — and it introduces the one category of error that is hardest to catch, which is a correct process applied to a mistyped number.

What we automate

Document Generation

Agreements, addenda, disclosures, and internal forms rendered from live data. Change the underlying record and the next rendering is right, rather than the document and the record disagreeing quietly.

Electronic Signature

Signature request, capture, and countersignature with an ESIGN-compliant consent disclosure. A material change to a document re-triggers consent; an editorial correction does not, because treating every typo as a new agreement trains people to click through.

Executed Copy Retention

The executed artifact is stored so it can be produced again intact — the document as signed, the signature, the consent record, and the timestamps. A signature you cannot retrieve is not evidence of anything.

Form Fill

Scan a recurring PDF once, map its fields to data the system already holds, and fill it on demand. Lender packets, state forms, OEM submissions, and the internal paperwork that has always been typed.

Approval Routing

Deal approvals, expense authorizations, discount exceptions, and policy sign-offs routed by rule rather than by walking a folder across the showroom, with the current holder always visible.

Month-End Packages

The reporting package, the DOC report, and the recurring lender and OEM submissions generated on a schedule from reconciled data. The assembly step disappears; the review step stays, because it should.

Deal Jacket Completeness

Required-document checklists per deal type, with what is missing surfaced while the customer is still reachable rather than during an audit six months later.

Notifications and Escalation

Alerts that go to a person with an action, not to a distribution list. Anything unactioned escalates on a defined clock instead of quietly sitting.

How we choose what to automate first

Not every manual task is worth removing, and automating a bad process makes it faster rather than better. During assessment we time the recurring work and rank it on three things: hours consumed, error consequence, and how stable the process is. High-hour, high-consequence, stable processes go first. Anything still changing every month waits, because building automation around a moving target produces something that has to be rebuilt.

Some things should stay manual. A review step exists to have a person look at the number. We automate assembly and leave judgment alone — a month-end package that publishes with nobody reading it has removed the wrong step.

Compliance posture

  • Consent is real. The ESIGN disclosure is presented and recorded, not assumed by a checkbox buried in a flow.
  • Versions are pinned. A signature attaches to the exact document version signed, so a later template edit cannot retroactively change what someone agreed to.
  • The audit trail is complete. Every generation, send, view, signature, and countersignature is recorded with actor and timestamp.
  • Retention is deliberate. Retention periods are configured per document type to your policy and your state's requirements rather than defaulting to keep-everything-forever.
  • Access is scoped. Who can generate, send, and countersign each document type is a role, and it is enforced on the server rather than by hiding a button.

We build the mechanism and the audit trail. We are not your compliance counsel — the policy those mechanisms enforce is yours and should be reviewed by someone qualified to review it.

Delivery

Workflow engagements are deliberately incremental. We take the two or three highest-cost processes, ship them, measure the hours actually recovered, and then decide what is next with real numbers rather than with the estimate we started from. First releases are typically live in four to eight weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we still need DocuSign or a similar service?
▼

For general business signatures many clients keep what they have. What we build is signature attached to your data and your records — a document rendered from the live system, signed, and retained with its audit trail in the same place the deal lives. Where that integration is the point, a separate signature service adds a hop without adding anything.

Can you automate our state title and registration paperwork?
▼

The form-fill mechanism handles any recurring PDF: scan it once, map the fields, and fill it from data the system already holds. Where a state offers electronic submission we can look at integrating it, but that is state by state and we scope it specifically rather than promising it in general.

What if our process changes after you build the automation?
▼

Templates, routing rules, and field mappings are configuration rather than code, so ordinary changes are made by your team without a development cycle. A structural change — a new document type, a new approval path — is scoped work. This is also why we deliberately do not automate processes that are still in flux.

How do you handle documents that need a wet signature?
▼

They stay on paper, and the system tracks them rather than pretending otherwise. The document is generated with live data, marked as requiring physical signature, and the executed copy is scanned back in against the record so the deal jacket is complete in one place even when part of it started as paper.

Will this integrate with our existing document storage?
▼

Usually. If you have a document management system you intend to keep, we write to it and reference from it rather than creating a second archive. Two places where a signed document might live is worse than one place with a slightly awkward interface.

Which task would you stop doing tomorrow?

Most stores can name it immediately. Tell us yours and we will tell you what removing it takes.
